# Quadratic covers of the Hermitian curve at the fixed binary target

This research note concerns ordinary functional AG codes over
\(\mathbb F_{4096}\), followed by the binary affine code
\([2048,12,1024]\). It proves a dimension ceiling for every quadratic cover
of the Hermitian curve in this architecture and computes one concrete
Garcia–Stichtenoth intermediate curve. The latter construction has dimension
432 and is not a leaderboard submission.

## 1. A dimension ceiling

Write \(H/\mathbb F_{4096}\) for the smooth plane Hermitian curve
\[
y^{64}+y=x^{65}.
\]
Its plane degree is 65, its genus is \(64\cdot63/2=2016\), and it has
\(64^3+1=262145\) rational points.

**Lemma 1.** If a smooth geometrically integral curve over \(\mathbb F_Q\)
has genus \(g\), \(N\) rational points, and \(S=N-Q-1\), then
\[
2Qg^2+(Q^2-Q-S)g-S^2\ge0.
\]

*Proof.* Pair the Frobenius eigenvalues and write their real pair sums as
\(t_1,\ldots,t_g\). The Weil theorem gives
\(\sum_i t_i=-S\) and
\(N_2=Q^2+1+2Qg-\sum_i t_i^2\). Since \(N_2\ge N\) and
\(\sum_i t_i^2\ge S^2/g\), multiplication by \(g\) gives the assertion.
The genus-zero case follows directly. This is the usual second-moment
derivation of Ihara's finite bound. ∎

**Lemma 2.** Every divisor of degree at most 225 on \(H\) has at most ten
independent sections. Every divisor of degree at most 512 on \(H\) has at
most 37 independent sections.

*Proof.* Noether's theorem for a smooth plane curve of degree \(d\), in its
arbitrary-characteristic form, says that for \(m=jd-i\), \(0\le i<d\),
\[
h^0(D)\le
\begin{cases}
j(j+1)/2,&i>j,\\
(j+1)(j+2)/2-i,&0\le i\le j.
\end{cases}
\]
Here all degrees are below the canonical degree \(65\cdot62=4030\).
For 225, use \(225=4\cdot65-35\), obtaining \(4\cdot5/2=10\).
For 512, use \(512=8\cdot65-8\), obtaining
\(9\cdot10/2-8=37\). Smaller degrees cannot have more sections, by
adding a suitable multiple of a rational point.

The precise reference is R. Hartshorne, *Generalized divisors on Gorenstein
curves and a theorem of Noether*, J. Math. Kyoto Univ. **26** (1986),
375–386, Theorem 2.1, DOI
[10.1215/kjm/1250520873](https://doi.org/10.1215/kjm/1250520873).
The formula and its applicability over every perfect field are stated in
R. Pellikaan, *On special divisors and the two variable zeta function*,
Theorem 2.2 and Remark 2.3, pp. 2–3 of the
[author's corrected version](https://ruudp.win.tue.nl/paper/22.pdf). ∎

**Lemma 3.** Let \(\pi:Y\to H\) be a degree-two finite morphism of smooth
geometrically integral curves over \(\mathbb F_{4096}\). If
\(\#Y(\mathbb F_{4096})\ge523264\), then every divisor \(G\) of degree
at most 1024 on \(Y\) satisfies \(h^0(G)\le37\).

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 1 with \(Q=4096\), \(N=523264\), and
\(S=519167\). At \(g=4829\), its left side is \(-12812580\); at
\(g=4830\), it is \(82567901\). The quadratic is increasing for
\(g\ge0\), and its required genus increases with \(N\) in this range.
Therefore \(g_Y\ge4830\). If the evaluation construction reserves a
rational pole, the slightly stronger point lower bound is 523265. At
\(S=519168\) the same two evaluations are \(-13855744\) and
\(81524736\), so the genus conclusion is unchanged.

If \(G\) has a section, replace it by a linearly equivalent effective
divisor. Put \(M=\deg G\) and \(\tau=g_Y-2g_H+1\ge799\).
The finite map between smooth curves is flat, so
\(E=\pi_*\mathcal O_Y(G)\) is a rank-two vector bundle. Equality of
Euler characteristics and Riemann–Roch give
\[
\deg E=M-\tau.
\]
Saturate the copy of \(\mathcal O_H\) generated by the section 1 inside
\(E\). Its saturation is \(\mathcal O_H(D)\), with \(D\) effective,
and the quotient \(L\) is a line bundle. Locally the saturation consists
of functions in the base function field whose pullback has poles bounded
by \(G\); hence \(\pi^*D\le G\) and \(\deg D\le M/2\le512\).
Consequently
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_H(D)\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow L
\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\deg L=M-\tau-\deg D.
\]
If \(h^0(L)=0\), Lemma 2 gives \(h^0(E)=h^0(D)\le37\).
Otherwise \(\deg L\ge0\). Both \(\deg D\) and \(\deg L\) are
then at most \(M-\tau\le225\), so Lemma 2 and the exact sequence give
\(h^0(E)\le10+10=20\). Since \(h^0(E)=h^0(G)\), both cases prove
the claim. ∎

For an outer functional code evaluated at \(N\le524288\) distinct
rational points, the usual degree certificate followed by the affine inner
code gives distance at least \(1024(N-\deg G)\). Meeting the specified
binary distance requires
\[
N-\deg G\ge535822336/1024=523264.
\]
For a nonzero outer code, \(\deg G\ge0\). Thus \(N\ge523264\) and \(\deg G\le1024\); Lemma 3 applies and
the binary dimension is at most \(12\cdot37=444\). This conclusion
allows arbitrary divisors, including multipoint divisors and poles of
higher degree, and arbitrary quadratic covers of \(H\). Its scope is
the stated degree-based functional-code certificate; it makes no claim
about other distance certificates or other outer architectures.

## 2. An exact Garcia–Stichtenoth intermediate

Set \(u=y/x\) on \(H\), and let \(Y\) be the smooth projective curve
with function field
\[
\mathbb F_{4096}(x,y,w),\qquad
y^{64}+y=x^{65},\qquad w^2+w=u^{65}.
\]
This is the quadratic intermediate between levels two and three of the
first Garcia–Stichtenoth tower. Indeed, the third level adjoins \(z\)
with \(z^{64}+z=u^{65}\), and
\(w=z+z^2+z^4+z^8+z^{16}+z^{32}\) satisfies the displayed quadratic
equation. The first-tower recursion and its elementary abelian
intermediate levels are given in S. Ballet, J. Pieltant, M. Rambaud and
J. Sijsling, *On some bounds for symmetric tensor rank of multiplication
in finite fields*, §2.1.1, p. 6 of the
[author version](https://perso.telecom-paristech.fr/rambaud/articles/BPRS.pdf).
The following finite calculations use the equations directly.

The function \(u\) has exactly 64 simple poles: the 63 affine points
\((0,\beta)\), \(\beta\in\mathbb F_{64}^{\times}\), and the point
at infinity \(P_\infty\). At the origin, \(v(x)=1\), \(v(y)=65\),
so \(u\) has a zero of order 64. At infinity,
\(v(x)=-64\), \(v(y)=-65\). Thus \(u^{65}\) has 64 poles, all of
odd order 65. Artin–Schreier ramification gives total ramification and
different exponent 66 at each of them, and no ramification elsewhere.
In particular the extension is a genuine degree-two extension. Hurwitz
gives
\[
2g_Y-2=2(2\cdot2016-2)+64\cdot66=8060+4224=12284,
\qquad g_Y=6143.
\]
The ramification formula used here is the usual Artin–Schreier formula,
e.g. H. Stichtenoth, *Algebraic Function Fields and Codes*, second edition,
Springer 2009, Proposition 3.7.8.

For each \(x\in\mathbb F_{4096}^{\times}\), there are 64 nonzero
solutions \(y\). Their values \(u\) are nonzero, and
\(u^{65}\in\mathbb F_{64}\), so their absolute traces to
\(\mathbb F_2\) vanish. All these points split in the quadratic
extension. The origin also splits, into the points \(w=0,1\). Each
of the 64 ramified rational points has one rational point above it.
Therefore
\[
\#Y(\mathbb F_{4096})=(4095\cdot64)\cdot2+2+64=524226.
\]
Write \(Q_\infty\) for the unique point above \(P_\infty\).

**Lemma 4.** The space \(\mathcal L_Y(961Q_\infty)\) has basis
\(\{x^iy^j:i,j\ge0,\ i+j\le7\}\).

*Proof.* Every function is uniquely \(A+Bw\), with \(A,B\in\mathbb
F_{4096}(H)\). At a ramified place the two terms have different valuation
parities, since \(v(w)=-65\). Regularity at each of the 63 finite
ramified places therefore forces \(A\) to be regular and \(B\) to
have valuation at least 33 on the base. At every other finite base place,
the involution \(\sigma(w)=w+1\) preserves regularity, and
\(B=f+\sigma(f)\) is regular. Since \(w\) is integral there,
\(A=f+Bw\) is regular as well.

At infinity, the bound 961 gives \(v(B)\ge-448\). A nonzero such
\(B\) would have at least \(63\cdot33=2079\) zeros and at most
448 poles, which is impossible. Thus \(B=0\), and
\(A\in\mathcal L_H(480P_\infty)\).
The Hermitian one-point basis consists of monomials of weights
\(64i+65j\), with \(0\le j<64\). All monomials with total degree
at most 7 have weight at most 455; any monomial with total degree at
least 8 has weight at least 512. This proves the stated basis. ∎

Evaluate these 36 monomials at all 524225 rational points of \(Y\)
except \(Q_\infty\). Their maximum pole order on \(Y\) is
\(2\cdot455=910\). Hence the outer code is linear over
\(\mathbb F_{4096}\), has dimension 36, and has minimum distance at
least \(524225-910=523315\). Evaluation is injective because a nonzero
function with poles of degree at most 910 cannot vanish at 524225
distinct places.

Choose a binary basis of \(\mathbb F_{4096}\) and identify a field
symbol with \((a_0,\ldots,a_{11})\in\mathbb F_2^{12}\). Map it to
the 2048 values
\[
(a_0+\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{11}a_i t_i)_{t\in\mathbb F_2^{11}}.
\]
This is the injective binary affine code \(\operatorname{RM}(1,11)\)
of dimension 12 and minimum distance 1024. Concatenate and append
\(63\cdot2048=129024\) zero coordinates. The resulting binary code
has
\[
\begin{aligned}
n&=(524225+63)\cdot2048=524288\cdot2048=1073741824,\\
k&=36\cdot12=432,\\
d_{\min}&\ge523315\cdot1024=535874560
         =535822336+52224.
\end{aligned}
\]

### Deterministic matrix generation

Use a polynomial basis of \(\mathbb F_2[T]/(p(T))\), where \(p\) is
the lexicographically first monic irreducible polynomial of degree 12.
Testing at most \(2^{12}\) candidates by the deterministic polynomial
gcd irreducibility criterion takes polynomial time in \(2^{12}\).
This is only a field-representation step; it involves no search over codes.

All basis functions are pulled back from \(H\), so it is unnecessary to
compute the extension coordinate \(w\). Enumerate the affine Hermitian
points in polynomial-basis order. Use each point with \(x\ne0\) twice,
the origin twice, and each \((0,\beta)\), \(\beta\ne0\), once.
This list has 524225 entries and gives precisely the required function
evaluations on \(Y\setminus\{Q_\infty\}\).

```text
Construct F = F_2[T]/(p(T)) and its ordered basis e_0,...,e_11.
L = empty list.
For every x,y in F, in the fixed order:
    If y^64 + y = x^65:
        Append (x,y) to L.
        If x != 0 or y = 0, append (x,y) once more.
For every (i,j) with i,j >= 0 and i+j <= 7, in lexicographic order:
    For b = 0,...,11:
        For (x,y) in L:
            Expand e_b*x^i*y^j = sum(a_h*e_h, h=0,...,11).
            For t in F_2^11, in lexicographic order:
                Output a_0 + sum(a_h*t_h, h=1,...,11).
        Output 129024 zero entries and end this matrix row.
```

The algorithm writes exactly \(432\cdot1073741824\) binary entries.
The point enumeration examines \(4096^2=16777216\) pairs. Field
arithmetic using polynomial bases costs \(O((\log n)^2)\) bit
operations per multiplication; the bounded exponents introduce only
another fixed or logarithmic factor. Matrix generation requires
\(O(kn\log n)\) elementary binary operations plus polynomial field
preprocessing. In the natural parameterized implementation,
\(4096\le n\), \(k\le n\), and \(\log n\le n\), so a conservative
uniform bound is \(O(n^4)\). No optimization or distance search is part
of the algorithm.
